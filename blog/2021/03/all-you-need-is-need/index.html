Being in The Way

All You Need Is Need

21 March 2021 · 2021 archive

"For God has committed them all to disobedience, that He might have mercy on all." — Romans 11:32 NKJV

There's an old story about two men out hunting on the open plains of Texas.

Far off on the horizon they see smoke. Then they hear it crackling. The wind picks up, and they realize a brush fire is racing toward them. Too fast to outrun.

One of them starts digging through his pockets and his pack. He finds a book of matches. And to his friend's amazement, he lights a fire right where they're standing.

It burns a wide black circle around them. They step into the middle of it, cover their faces and wait.

The wildfire roars up… and passes right over them. Not a burn.

Fire can't burn where fire has already burned.

The burned-over place

The law is like that wildfire. It levels everything. "All have sinned and fall short" (Romans 3:23). Nobody outruns it.

But there is a place where the fire has already burned. The cross.

That's where judgment already fell, on Him. That's where we stand. Hardly believing it, but safe.

Christ's death disarmed the law. Where there used to be guilt, now there's only gratitude.

All you need is need

Tim Keller put it this way: if you want God's grace, all you need is need.

That sounds easy. It isn't.

We come to God saying, Look at all I've done. Or, Look at all I've suffered. We think God loves strength. So we hide our frailty.

But need is what draws the compassion of Christ. He is near "to those who have a broken heart" (Psalm 34:18).

Paul begged three times for his thorn to be taken away. The answer was, "My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness." So Paul said, "Most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me" (2 Corinthians 12:8–9).

Nature hates a vacuum

Aristotle said nature abhors a vacuum. Every empty space gets filled with something.

The soul is the same. Leave an empty place in the heart, and the flesh will rush in to fill it.

Pride is refusing to admit the need. Pretending the space isn't empty. And "God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble" (James 4:6).

He comes looking

In Luke 15 a coin rolls out of a woman's hand and under the furniture. A coin can't cry for help. It just lies there, lost.

And she lights a lamp and sweeps the whole house until she finds it.

In God's math, ninety-nine sheep is not "plenty of sheep." He goes after the one.

You don't have to be strong to be found. You just have to be lost.

What need are you hiding from Him today?
