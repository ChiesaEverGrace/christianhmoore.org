Being in The Way

Secure Enough to Wash Feet

10 October 2021 · 2021 archive

"Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into His hands, and that He had come from God and was going to God, rose from supper and laid aside His garments, took a towel and girded Himself." — John 13:3–4 NKJV

The first Adam had everything. A perfect garden. Perfect fellowship with God.

And still he became insecure in the love of God.

"They were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed" (Genesis 2:25).

Then, after the fall: "I heard Your voice in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked; and I hid myself" (Genesis 3:10).

Fear, shame and control

Sin brought three things with it.

Fear. Guilty fear is about what I've done.

Shame. Shame is about who I am. Something is wrong with me. I'm exposed. I need to find something to cover myself.

Control. Control is how I react to feeling exposed. If I can just grab the wheel, maybe I won't be hurt again.

We know this one.

The Second Adam

Now look at Jesus in the upper room.

The disciples had just been arguing about which of them was the greatest (Luke 22:24). Judas already had betrayal in his heart. The cross was hours away. Jesus knew all of it.

And He knew three other things.

The Father had given all things into His hands. He had come from God. He was going back to God.

So He got up, took off His outer garment, and picked up a towel.

He could afford to be the servant. He was secure. He "did not consider it robbery to be equal with God" and so He "made Himself of no reputation, taking the form of a bondservant" (Philippians 2:6–7).

He could afford to be vulnerable. No fig leaves. No covering up. Even in a hostile room.

Peter grabs the wheel

Then He comes to Peter.

"Lord, are You washing my feet?" "What I am doing you do not understand now, but you will know after this." "You shall never wash my feet!"

Peter felt the gap between himself and his Lord. He felt unworthy. Ashamed. So he snapped to control.

Then Jesus said, "If I do not wash you, you have no part with Me."

And Peter let go. "Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head!"

He gave up control for communion.

So what do we do?

We can let go of control. We know what the Father has given us. We know where we stand in His love, in the Finished Work. And we know where we're going. God is in control. Not people. Not my flesh. Not anyone else's.

We're free to enjoy communion with Him.

The Body of Christ can be a shame-free place. No shaming each other.

And we are washed. "He who is bathed needs only to wash his feet, but is completely clean; and you are clean" (John 13:10).

What are you gripping today that He's asking you to let go of?
