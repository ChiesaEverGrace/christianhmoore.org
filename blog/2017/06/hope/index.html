Being in The Way

Hope

2017 · 2017 archive

"Therefore gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and rest your hope fully upon the grace that is to be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ." — 1 Peter 1:13 NKJV

Where do you put your hope when the trouble comes anyway?

We say we trust God. Then the diagnosis comes. The betrayal. The door that closes. The plan that falls apart.
And something in us whispers, If God really loved me, this wouldn't be happening.

We know that voice.

The oldest misreading

It's not a new voice. Satan used it on Jesus.

In the wilderness he quoted Psalm 91. "He shall give His angels charge over you… lest you dash your foot against a stone" (Luke 4:10–11).

In other words: If You are the Son of God, nothing can hurt You. Prove it.

It's the same lie he still tells us. If God loves you, you won't suffer. So when suffering comes, it must mean He doesn't.

That's the strategy. Promise a pain-free life, and the first pain becomes a reason to doubt.

Psalm 91 promises refuge.
It never promised immunity.

Not a hair

Listen to what Jesus actually told His disciples.

"You will be betrayed even by parents and brothers, relatives and friends; and they will put some of you to death. And you will be hated by all for My name's sake. But not a hair of your head shall be lost. By your patience possess your souls." (Luke 21:16–19)

Read that again.

Some of you will be betrayed.
Some of you will be killed.
And not a hair of your head will be lost.

How can both be true?

A hair is the smallest thing you have. The most fragile. The easiest to lose. And God says even that is kept.

No part of your life, your identity or your destiny in Christ will ever be lost. All of it is held in His resurrection power.

That's what safety means for a believer. Not that nothing touches us. That nothing that is truly ours can be taken.

Safe doesn't always look safe

Joseph was sold by his brothers, lied about, forgotten in a prison.
None of that looked safe.

Years later he could say it: "You meant evil against me; but God meant it for good… to save many people alive" (Genesis 50:20).

Safety can look unsafe in the moment and clear in hindsight.

"All things work together for good to those who love God" (Romans 8:28).
Not all things are good.
But He works all things together for good.

What you behold, you become

Pastor Carl Stevens used to say that the believer becomes like the object of his occupation.

In your trial, don't take on the image of your trial. Behold Him.

"But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

If I stare at the storm, I start to look like the storm.
If I look at Jesus, I start to look like Him.

Under His wings

"He shall cover you with His feathers, and under His wings you shall take refuge" (Psalm 91:4).

Think of a mother bird. When the storm or the predator comes, she doesn't move the chicks out of the storm. She spreads her wings and takes it on herself.

That's the cross.
Jesus took the storm. Jesus took the predator.
So what comes to us now is refining, not punishment.

And many of His promises are kept in full on the far side of the resurrection. What looks like a contradiction today resolves in His death and rising.

So what do we do?

By your patience possess your souls.

If anything matters more to me than God, I'm not safe. Because if I lose that thing, it undoes me.

When I love Him most, I'm finally secure.

God protects us through trouble, not always from it.
His presence is our safety.
Our soul is kept.
And our suffering has a purpose.

So rest your hope fully on Him.

What are you holding onto today that would undo you if you lost it?
