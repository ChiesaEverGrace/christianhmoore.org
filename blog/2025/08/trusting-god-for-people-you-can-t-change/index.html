Being in The Way

Trusting God for People You Can't Change

August 2025 · 2025 archive

"The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD, like the rivers of water; He turns it wherever He wishes." — Proverbs 21:1 NKJV

There are people in your life you would change if you could.

A son. A daughter. A husband or wife. A brother in the church. Someone who opposes you.

We can't change them. But we try. We push. We nag. We manage.

That's control. And control is unbelief in disguise.

It comes from fear, or pride, or not really believing God is in charge. And it doesn't work. Control can't touch the heart. Only God can.

Faith releases people into God's plan. Control tries to make them fit ours.

Our children

Abraham laid Isaac on the altar (Genesis 22). Job got up early and offered sacrifices for each of his children (Job 1:5). Jochebed put her baby in a basket and set him in the Nile (Exodus 2:3).

Think about that. She let go of Moses. And Pharaoh's own daughter picked him up, raised him, educated him, protected him (Acts 7:21–22). Later God made him Israel's deliverer.

Parenting isn't control. It's stewardship. God's grace will keep our children far beyond what our fear ever could.

Only God can shape their hearts. So trade manipulation for prayer. Patience. Surrender.

Intercession

Abraham prayed for Lot (Genesis 18). Moses stood in the gap for Israel (Exodus 32). Paul said, "My heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that they may be saved" (Romans 10:1).

Prayer is trust in action. It puts people in God's hands, where our hands can't reach.

When Lot grabbed the best land, Abraham let him go. And God said to Abraham, "Lift your eyes now… all the land which you see I give to you" (Genesis 13:14–15).

Control smothers. Prayer entrusts.

Those who oppose us

Jesus prayed for the ones nailing Him to the cross (Luke 23:34). Stephen prayed for the men stoning him (Acts 7:60). David had Saul in his hands and wouldn't touch him. "Let the LORD judge between you and me" (1 Samuel 24:12).

The flesh wants to fight back. Or to manipulate the outcome.

But only God can convict an enemy. Trust hands them over to Him, and sets us free to forgive.

Seeing each other in Christ

"From now on, we regard no one according to the flesh" (2 Corinthians 5:16).

When I see you "in the flesh," I see your failures and weaknesses. And I want to control you.

When Christ stands between us, I see the Finished Work first. I'm not trusting your performance. I'm trusting what He did. Love sees Calvary first.

The Body and the ministry

"I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6). When Paul said goodbye to the Ephesian elders, he committed them "to God and to the word of His grace" (Acts 20:32).

Leaders get tempted to control people into growth or loyalty. That produces fear and legalism. Christ is the Shepherd. Let the Spirit do the changing.

Jeremiah preached faithfully and few listened. Jesus watched many walk away in John 6 and turned to the twelve: "Do you also want to go away?"

We're responsible for faithfulness. Not for results. "Your labor is not in vain in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 15:58). His word "shall not return to Me void" (Isaiah 55:11).

Trust, release, prosper

Over and over in Scripture, when someone let go and trusted God, God prospered it. Not always with money. With His purposes fulfilled. Lives preserved. Fruit multiplied.

We pray. We plant. We water. He gives the increase.

Who do you need to put back in His hands today?
