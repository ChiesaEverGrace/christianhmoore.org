Being in The Way

Who Told You That You Were Naked?

6 August 2022 · 2022 archive

"Then the LORD God called to Adam and said to him, 'Where are you?'" — Genesis 3:9 NKJV

Something happens to us. A loss. A betrayal. A failure.

And we feel exposed. Uncovered.

That feeling has a name. Shame. Something is wrong with me. I'm not worthy of love. Not worthy of grace.

The fig leaves

So we make a quiet vow. I will never let that happen to me again.

And we start sewing fig leaves. Layer after layer of protection. A self we show to people. A false self.

It works, for a while. Nobody gets close enough to hurt us.

But nobody gets close enough to love us either. We stop being honest. We stop being vulnerable. And we stop receiving grace.

Then God walks into the garden.

"Where are you?" "I was afraid because I was naked; and I hid myself." "Who told you that you were naked?" (Genesis 3:9–11)

He didn't come to expose Adam. He came looking for him.

A different kind of exposure

Isaiah saw the Lord, high and lifted up. The seraphim cried, "Holy, holy, holy." The doorposts shook (Isaiah 6:1–4).

And Isaiah was undone. "Woe is me… because I am a man of unclean lips" (6:5).

That's exposure too. But watch what God does with it.

A seraph flies to him with a coal from the altar and touches his mouth. "Your iniquity is taken away, and your sin purged" (6:7).

Then a voice: "Whom shall I send?"

And the man who a moment ago was undone says, "Here am I! Send me" (6:8).

Shame hides. Grace sends.

When we live outside our gospel identity

An identity crisis becomes a mission crisis. We stop going.

We swing between shame and self-love. Either way, we're occupied with ourselves.

And we lose our focus, because we've lost our consecration.

Who you really are

Your identity in the gospel isn't built on opinion. Not other people's. Not even your own. It's a gift, from Someone outside of you.

The gospel frees you from what others think of you. It also frees you from what you think of yourself.

You aren't who they say you are. You aren't who you say you are. You are who God says you are. His beloved child, in whom He is well pleased.

That means you don't have to perform to earn His love. In Christ you already have it. His righteousness is counted as yours, as your sin was counted as His.

The old you, the things that used to "make" you, was buried with Him. "Buried with Him in baptism, in which you also were raised with Him through faith" (Colossians 2:12).

Free to forget yourself

Here's the irony. The more we worry about our performance, the worse we perform. Constant self-inspection just makes us more self-centered.

C.S. Lewis said you'd recognize a truly humble man because he isn't thinking about humility. He isn't thinking about himself at all.

When your significance, security and identity are locked up in Christ, you don't have to win. You're free to lose.

"For to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (Philippians 1:21).

Who told you that you were naked?
