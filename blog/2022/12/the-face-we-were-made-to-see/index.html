Being in The Way

The Face We Were Made to See

28 December 2022 · 2022 archive

"And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth." — John 1:14 NKJV

We are always looking.

At the sunset. At the screen. At the next place, the next thing, the next face.

Greg Morse calls them "the starving eyes of man." I can't get that phrase out of my head. Because it's true. Our eyes are hungry.

Hungry from the start

The first sin started with a look. "When the woman saw that the tree was… pleasant to the eyes… she took of its fruit and ate" (Genesis 3:6).

And the eyes have never been full since. "Hell and Destruction are never full; so the eyes of man are never satisfied" (Proverbs 27:20).

We climb the mountain. We see the view. We take the picture. A week later we're scrolling for the next one.

It's not that we look too much. It's that we keep looking at things too small for the hunger.

Show me Your glory

Moses knew what he was hungry for. "Please, show me Your glory" (Exodus 33:18).

David too. "One thing I have desired… to behold the beauty of the LORD" (Psalm 27:4).

But here was the problem. The one sight that could satisfy them was the one sight they couldn't survive. "You cannot see My face; for no man shall see Me, and live" (Exodus 33:20).

So God put Moses in the cleft of the rock, covered him with His hand, and passed by. Moses saw His back. He heard His Name. But not the face.

Think of staring at the sun. The problem isn't the sun. It's our eyes.

The face in the manger

Then, one night in Bethlehem, the face that Moses couldn't look at was lying in a feeding trough.

"No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, He has declared Him" (John 1:18).

God who said, "Let light shine out of darkness," has shone in our hearts "to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6).

In the face of Jesus.

The glory came close enough to look at. Wrapped in flesh. Veiled, the way Moses veiled his face when he came down the mountain. "He has no form or comeliness… no beauty that we should desire Him" (Isaiah 53:2).

Most people looked right at Him and saw a carpenter.

Even Philip said, "Lord, show us the Father." And Jesus said, "He who has seen Me has seen the Father" (John 14:8–9).

They will see His face

The night before the cross, Jesus prayed this: "Father, I desire that they also whom You gave Me may be with Me where I am, that they may behold My glory" (John 17:24).

He wants us to see Him.

And the Bible ends with the promise. "They shall see His face" (Revelation 22:4).

No more cleft in the rock. No more back. No more veil.

The Puritan Jeremiah Burroughs said that looking at something beautiful doesn't make a man beautiful. But seeing God makes the soul glorious.

"We shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is" (1 John 3:2).

So where are you looking?

This Christmas, every window and every screen will be competing for your eyes.

They can feed the hunger for a moment. They can't fill it.

Look at the manger. Look at the cross. Look at Him.

The face your eyes were made for has come close.
