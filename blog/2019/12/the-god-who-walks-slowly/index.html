Being in The Way

The God Who Walks Slowly

29 December 2019 · 2019 archive

"While they conversed and reasoned, that Jesus Himself drew near and went with them." — Luke 24:15 NKJV

Three days after the cross, two disciples are walking home to Emmaus. Seven miles. Heads down.

And Jesus Himself comes up beside them. They don't recognize Him.

"What kind of conversation is this that you have with one another as you walk and are sad?"

"We were hoping that it was He who was going to redeem Israel…" (24:17, 21).

We were hoping. Past tense.

The slow work of God

Notice what Jesus doesn't do.

He doesn't rush. He isn't anxious about their unbelief. He isn't fretting about the disciples who haven't figured it out yet.

He walks with them. At their pace. And He opens the Scriptures, beginning with Moses, all the way through.

The Japanese theologian Kosuke Koyama wrote about the "three mile an hour God." God walks slowly, he said, because He is love. Love has its own speed. Not the speed of technology. The speed of walking. The speed of a friend.

He's slow. But He's never late.

Getting us alone

God's slow pace often gets us alone with Him.

We don't always like that. "Loved one and friend You have put far from me, and my acquaintances into darkness" (Psalm 88:18).

When it gets quiet, things come up. The pretending. The self-importance. The parts of us we've been running from. There's nowhere to hide.

So we push back. We fill the silence. We scroll.

They call it FOMO now. The fear of missing out. It's in the dictionary. We live our lives through a filter, measuring ourselves against everybody else's highlights.

And while we're looking out at the crowd, we lose sight of who we actually are in Christ.

Present in His presence

"But the LORD is in His holy temple. Let all the earth keep silence before Him" (Habakkuk 2:20).

An acoustic ecologist named Gordon Hempton has spent years looking for places with no human noise at all. He says there are almost none left.

Silence is rare. But silence is where we notice Him.

Silence is simply acknowledging that God is here, without interruption. It makes room to meet with Him.

Someone described prayer as being willing, every day, to stand at the door and wait.

He was known in the breaking of bread

When they got to Emmaus, Jesus acted as if He'd keep going. They begged Him, "Abide with us, for it is toward evening."

So He went in. At the table He took bread, blessed it, broke it and gave it to them.

"Then their eyes were opened and they knew Him" (24:31).

When we let God slow us down, three things happen. We face the darkness we've been running from, but we face it in His nearness and grace. Our anxiety starts to heal. And at His table we recognize Him. He's been walking with us the whole way.

What would it look like to walk at His pace this week?
