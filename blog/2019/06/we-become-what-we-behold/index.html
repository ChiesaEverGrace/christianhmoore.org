Being in The Way

We Become What We Behold

2019 · 2019 archive

"But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord." — 2 Corinthians 3:18 NKJV

What are you looking at?

It matters more than we think. Because we become what we behold.

What we look at shapes how we see

Jesus said, "The lamp of the body is the eye. If therefore your eye is good, your whole body will be full of light" (Matthew 6:22).

Proverbs talks about two kinds of eyes.
The evil eye, always hungry for more (Proverbs 28:22).
The bountiful eye, that sees the need and gives (Proverbs 22:9).

Same world. Two completely different ways of seeing it.

What we look at moves the heart

Jeremiah wept over Jerusalem and said, "Mine eye affecteth mine heart" (Lamentations 3:51 KJV).

What goes in through the eyes doesn't stay there. It disturbs us. It challenges us. Sometimes it changes our direction.

What we love moves the mind

Paul wrote one sad line about a co-worker. "Demas has forsaken me, having loved this present world" (2 Timothy 4:10).

Demas didn't wake up one morning and decide to leave. He looked, and looked, and kept looking, until he loved what he was looking at.
And what he loved took him away.

The eye moves the heart.
The heart moves the mind.
And the mind moves the feet.

Where else would we go?

In John 6, Jesus said things that were hard to hear. "This is a hard saying; who can understand it?" (6:60)

"From that time many of His disciples went back and walked with Him no more" (6:66).

They heard words.
Peter heard something else.

Jesus turned to the twelve. "Do you also want to go away?"

And Peter said, "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life" (6:67–68).

The word there is rhēma. Not just information. A living word, spoken to you. Jesus had already said it: "The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life" (6:63).

The ones who left heard a hard saying.
Peter heard life.

What made the difference? What Peter had been looking at. He'd been beholding Jesus.

So what do we do?

Turn your eyes.

Not to the news.
Not to the trial.
Not to the present world.

To Him.

Because whatever you keep looking at, you will slowly become.

What has your eye been resting on this week?
