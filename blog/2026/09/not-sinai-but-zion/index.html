Being in The Way

Not Sinai, but Zion

26 September 2026 · 2026 archive

"But you have come to Mount Zion, and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem… and to Jesus, mediator of a new covenant, and to blood of sprinkling that speaks better things than Abel's." — Hebrews 12:22, 24 (OGX)

Hebrews 12 puts two mountains side by side.

One you've been brought out of. One you've been brought to.

Which one are you living on?

Sinai

"For you have not come to a mountain that can be touched, and to a fire that has been kindled, and to darkness, and to gloom, and to a whirlwind…" (12:18, OGX).

Sinai had a boundary rope. Cross it and you died. Animal or man.

Fire. Darkness. Gloom. Storm. The weight of God's holiness meeting sin, with no mediator in between.

A voice so terrifying the people begged God to stop speaking to them.

Even Moses, the man closest to God on that mountain, said, "I am exceedingly afraid and trembling" (12:21).

And when Moses came down, his face shone, and he had to cover it. Even the closest relationship under that covenant was veiled.

That's Sinai. A covenant of demand. Relationship at a distance. Kept up by performance.

Zion

"But you have come…"

Not "you're climbing." You have come. It's done. You're already there.

And look at the atmosphere. Not smoke and terror. Myriads of angels in a festal gathering. A celebration.

"The assembly of the firstborn ones, enrolled in the heavens." Firstborn. Full inheritance, by birth. Not by merit.

God is there as Judge of all. And yet we stand there with "the spirits of righteous ones made perfect." Holiness and access, together.

Jesus is there. Not hiding His face. Standing in the open as the One who made the way in.

And His blood speaks. Abel's blood cried out from the ground for justice against his brother. This blood speaks peace. Welcome.

That's Zion. A covenant of gift. Sustained by His Finished Work, not our performance.

Living at Sinai

You can be saved and still live like you're at Sinai.

In your own life it looks like managing distance. Always calculating. Have I done enough? Said enough? Repented enough?

In relationships it looks like unspoken law. Keeping score. Remembering debts. Pulling away the moment someone falls short.

In the church it looks like comparison. Who's more spiritual. More disciplined. More useful. And it ends in isolation, like Elijah leaving his servant behind and going off alone.

Living at Zion

Grace doesn't remove honesty. It doesn't remove correction. It removes the threat of exile behind it.

At Zion, love can correct without abandoning. Because the relationship was never secured by performance in the first place.

And fellowship stops being a performance review. Every one of us is a firstborn. Nobody is there to be measured against anybody else. We're all there because the same blood brought us to the same mountain.

What happened in Galatia

Paul names Sinai in Galatians 4. The church there had drifted from grace back to law.

Did they get holier? No. They got sharper.

"If you bite and devour one another, beware lest you be consumed by one another!" (Galatians 5:15).

Under Sinai, correction turns into accusation. Conviction turns into condemnation. Difference turns into division. And someone's failure becomes their identity instead of their history.

Legalism doesn't produce love. It produces a need to feel superior.

Paul's answer wasn't "try harder to be nice." It was, "Stand fast therefore in the liberty by which Christ has made us free" (5:1). "Walk in the Spirit" (5:16).

In other words, remember which mountain you live on.

You have not come to Sinai.

You have come to Zion.
