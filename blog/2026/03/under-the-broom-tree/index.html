Being in The Way

Under the Broom Tree

8 March 2026 · 2026 archive

"Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed earnestly." — James 5:17 NKJV

A man with a nature like ours.

We forget that about Elijah. We remember the fire from heaven. We forget the broom tree.

Anxiety usually comes from a deep belief: If this doesn't go the way it should, something terrible will happen, and I won't survive it.

It isn't just fear of discomfort. It's fear of devastation. And the enemy loves to use that moment of fear to define everything.

Anxiety rests on three lies.

Lie one: "It's over"

Elijah had just seen one of the greatest miracles in the Bible. Fire fell on Carmel. The prophets of Baal were defeated. The nation cried, "The LORD, He is God!"

Then one message came from Jezebel. By this time tomorrow, you're dead.

And he ran. A day's journey into the wilderness. He sat down under a broom tree and asked to die. "It is enough! Now, LORD, take my life" (1 Kings 19:4).

Why?

He expected the revival to keep going. He expected Jezebel to fall. When things went the other way, he felt it was all out of control. And if I'm not in control, I'm not safe.

The first lie: I have to control the outcome to be safe.

Lie two: "This defines me"

Elijah lay down under that tree and let the threat define him. He was ready to be done.

The second lie: If I suffer, it will define me or destroy me.

Now watch what God does. He doesn't start with correction.

He sends an angel who touches him. "Arise and eat." There's a cake baked on coals and a jar of water by his head. Elijah eats and sleeps again.

And the angel comes back a second time. Touches him again. "Arise and eat, because the journey is too great for you" (19:5–7).

Too great for you. God knows.

When the load feels too heavy, it's often because we've started carrying something that belongs to God alone.

And when we collapse, Jesus comes. He touches us. He feeds us.

Lie three: "I'm alone"

Later Elijah says it out loud. "I have been very zealous for the LORD… I alone am left; and they seek to take my life" (19:10).

The third lie: I'm alone in this.

God answers by showing Elijah his future.

There are seven thousand in Israel who never bowed to Baal. You're not alone. You have a Body.

You're going to anoint kings.

You're going to train Elisha.

Elijah thought his story was ending. God was opening the next chapter.

So what do we do?

Fear and anxiety will pierce us sometimes. We're human. Made of dust. It happens.

But what we do next is up to us.

We can stay under the broom tree.

Or we can get up. Eat what He's put in front of us. Find the seven thousand. Anoint kings. Disciple an Elisha.

Anxiety says, This moment defines everything.

God says, This moment is only a chapter.

What broom tree are you sitting under today?
