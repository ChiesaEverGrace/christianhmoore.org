Being in The Way

Two Lost Sons and a Waiting Father

3 May 2026 · 2026 archive

"A certain man had two sons." — Luke 15:11 NKJV

We call it the parable of the prodigal son.

But Jesus started it with two sons. And here's the shock. Both of them were lost.

Neither of them knew their father's heart. And each of them is one of us.

The younger son: lost in rebellion

"Father, give me the portion of goods that falls to me" (15:12).

He takes it, goes off to a far country and wastes it all.

He walks out of the house. He breaks the relationship in the open. He rejects his father's authority, spends his inheritance, and ends up empty.

His lostness is loud. Nobody could miss it.

What he's really saying is, I don't want you. I want what you can give me.

Then, in a pig pen, "he came to himself." My father's hired servants have more than enough bread, and I'm starving. I will arise and go to my father (15:17–18).

The elder son: lost in religion

The older brother is out in the field.

He never left home. He kept the rules. He worked hard every day.

But his heart was just as far away.

Listen to him when the party starts. "Look, these many years I have been serving you; I never transgressed your commandment at any time; and yet you never gave me a young goat" (15:29).

That isn't how a son talks. That's how a slave talks.

What he's really saying is, I stayed. But I never knew you.

Same lostness, two different roads

The younger son was far from his father in distance. The elder son was far from his father in heart.

One broke the rules. The other trusted the rules.

One sinned by rebellion. The other by self-righteousness.

But the root was the same. Both wanted the father's things more than the father.

One said, Give me what's mine now. The other said, You owe me for my obedience. Both were using their father. One through independence. One through performance.

You can be lost in the far country. And you can be lost in the father's house.

Lostness isn't only running from God. It's living near God and never knowing His heart.

Only one comes in

The younger son came home. The elder son stood outside and wouldn't go in.

The saddest thing in the story isn't the rebellion of the younger. It's the blindness of the elder.

Because the rebel knows he's lost. The religious man thinks he's fine.

The father's heart

The father goes out to both of them.

He runs to the rebel. He pleads with the religious one.

Because both misunderstood the same thing. What it means to be a son.

And look what he gives the one who came home. "Bring out the best robe and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet" (15:22).

The robe is who you are. Your shame is covered. Forgiven.

The ring is what you carry. Your father's authority. Your place in Christ.

The sandals are how you walk. In freedom. In grace.

The feast is where you belong. A place where you're celebrated. The family of God.

Which son are you today?

The Father is waiting for both.
