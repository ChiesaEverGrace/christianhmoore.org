Being in The Way

I Have No Man

25 March 2023 · 2023 archive

"Sir, I have no man to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up; but while I am coming, another steps down before me." — John 5:7 NKJV

How would you describe the perfect man?

We could try. But we aren't perfect. So we probably wouldn't recognize Him if He stood in front of us.

That's why, when Jesus does something in our lives, it's so often unexpected. Sometimes even disappointing, or disturbing. But it's Wonderful. Isaiah called Him that. "His name will be called Wonderful, Counselor" (Isaiah 9:6).

John 5 is one of those moments.

The pool

By the Sheep Gate in Jerusalem there was a pool called Bethesda, with five porches. And they were full. Blind, lame, paralyzed. All waiting for the water to move.

The story went that an angel stirred the water now and then, and the first one in got healed.

First come, first healed. So the quickest won. Or the ones with the most help.

That pool is a picture of our world. We all have a pool we're trying to get into. Our health back. A career that failed. A marriage that went wrong. A personal failure we want to make right.

The man

One man had been lying there thirty-eight years.

Jesus saw him and asked, "Do you want to be made well?"

It's not a cruel question. Jesus' questions bring out what's really in our hearts. Sometimes we surprise ourselves by what we say.

What did he say? Not "yes."

"I have no man."

Yes, but. That's our answer too. In our paralysis we assume what we need is help. Someone to work with us to improve things.

Maybe it's shame. Maybe we compare ourselves to others. Maybe we feel we've let people down. Maybe we just want to feel better about ourselves.

Here's the truth. Until we realize we're paralyzed, we aren't looking for a Savior. We're looking for a helping hand.

Everybody else at that pool had somebody. This man had nobody.

That's why Jesus chose him.

And really, none of us have a man either.

I am the pool

"Rise, take up your bed and walk" (5:8).

And he did. Immediately.

Thirty-eight years without walking. No therapy. No rehab. Just up.

Can you imagine? What?! Oh… Jesus didn't help him to the pool. He is the pool.

The controversy

It was the Sabbath. So the first thing the man hears isn't celebration. It's complaint. "It is not lawful for you to carry your bed."

Controversy is often the signature of a real miracle.

Later Jesus found him in the temple. "See, you have been made well. Sin no more, lest a worse thing come upon you" (5:14).

Why say that? Because you can have a miracle without a changed heart. You can get what you want from God, even something spectacular, and still not be transformed.

And then the man goes and tells the authorities it was Jesus. To save his own skin.

We want a tidy ending. Healed, converted, sent out to plant churches. We don't get one.

The nature of grace

So why did Jesus heal him?

Not because he had great faith. He didn't even know who Jesus was. Not because he promised anything. Not because he deserved it.

Jesus does what He does because He is good. Ridiculously, radically good.

That's the nature of grace.

Who are you waiting on to put you in the water?
